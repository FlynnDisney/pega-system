### Pride 1: Understanding Pride

**Subject:** Pride 1: Understanding Pride

**Body:**

You're receiving this email to develop your emotion of Pride.

Pride is the positive emotion related to motivation, specifically the motivation to seek greater challenges.

When you feel pride, you literally hold your head up high. That embodied confidence helps you manage anxiety, commit to your goals and stay true to yourself in any situation.

Authentic pride comes from appreciating your efforts, not your abilities. Research shows that recognising effort is what builds self-esteem and the willingness to keep trying, whereas attributing success to talent often leads to anti-social arrogance – and the fear of failure.

You'll feel the most pride when your efforts align with what your community values. In this way, pride strengthens both personal motivation and social connection.

In the next email, you'll learn a simple intervention for developing authentic pride.

### Pride 2: Finding pride in past achievements

**Subject:** Pride 2: Finding pride in past achievements

**Body:**

In the last email, you learnt that effort attributions are the key to authentic pride.

In this email, you're going to learn an intervention for practising the skill of recognising effort.

All you need is a pen and paper, and as little as five minutes.

Achievement:

Choose one achievement from the last year – it could be something like having a difficult conversation, maintaining a commitment or completing a project.

Initial attribution:

How did you first think about your success? For example – "Other people made it happen", "I was in the right place at the right time", "I'm naturally good at it".

Effort attribution:

What kind of effort did you have to make? What kind of physical or emotional barriers did you have to overcome?

Why it mattered:

How did this achievement benefit yourself, others or build your capacity to deal with future problems?

By completing this task, you're taking the first step towards developing the emotion of pride. Learning to recognise the value of your effort might feel strange – even cringe at first, but keep practicing it and you'll build authentic pride, confidence and motivation.

### Pride 3: Building pride into your routine

**Subject:** Pride 3: Building pride into your routine

**Body:**

In the last email, you practiced recognising how your efforts contributed to past achievements.

In this email, you're going to explore how your everyday or weekly activities can support the development of pride. Habits that you're proud of regularly contribute to your sense of self-esteem and build the authentic confidence that pride provides.

For this intervention, you need a pen and paper – and about five minutes.

What habits are you already doing that create pride?

List 2-3 habits where you consistently make an effort.

Next to these, write what you find effortful about performing them.

Now, imagine ONE new habit that you would be proud of.

Consider behaviours that you know would benefit your health, your household or your family.

Then, write what you would find challenging about these new behaviours.

Underneath, write 1-3 scripts that recognise your efforts. These are sentences that acknowledge your efforts, even if you didn't complete the task as you wanted, like "even though I didn't finish, I did my best". Write in reference to the challenges you've identified.

Perfection isn't what matters. It's the recognition of effort that counts towards pride — even if that effort is small. After about a month of conscious effort, habits become familiar. Until then, leveraging pride is a powerful way to make those efforts matter.

**Body:**
